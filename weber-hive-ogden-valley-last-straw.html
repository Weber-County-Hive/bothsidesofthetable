Ogden Valley · Weber County · Origin Story

The Last Straw

A development the county's own planning commission voted to deny. A commissioner who approved it anyway. A resident who called it exactly that. Three years later: a brand-new city, a state study that promised a surplus, and a 512.6% emergency tax vote to cover the gap the study said wouldn't exist.

How to read this

  1. Ogden Valley City is the new name for the previously unincorporated areas of Eden, Liberty, and Huntsville — not including Huntsville Town itself, which has been its own separately incorporated municipality since 1924 and remains so today.
  2. Every fact below is sourced to a public vote, a public filing, a news report, or a document Ogden Valley City itself has published.
  3. This isn't one story — it's the same handful of names and the same handful of documents, showing up again at each stage: the 2023 rezone, the 2024 incorporation vote, the 2026 general plan, and the 2026 tax crisis.
  4. Companion pieces with the full underlying research are linked at the bottom of this page.

The timeline

2016

The plan Ogden Valley still uses today

Weber County adopts the Ogden Valley General Plan. The consultant team includes Zions Bank Public Finance. The Planning Commission includes Steve Waldrip — years before he becomes Gov. Cox's senior housing adviser.

Nov. 14, 2023

The Planning Commission says no

The Ogden Valley Planning Commission votes 5-2 to recommend denying a rezone for Eden Crossing — a 20-acre, up-to-325-unit mixed-use development proposed for the heart of Eden's commercial center.

Dec. 5, 2023

The county approves it anyway

The Weber County Commission votes 2-1 to approve the Eden Crossing rezone, overriding its own advisory planning body. Commission Chair Gage Froerer is the lead public defender of the project.

"The rezoning is well thought out in anticipation of growth in Ogden Valley... It advocates for removing density off of the hillside and valley floors to put it into village areas that could be served by appropriate infrastructure." — Gage Froerer

Early 2024

"The last straw"

Incorporation advocate Wheatley names the Eden Crossing override as the turning point that fueled the push to make Ogden Valley its own city.

"The Eden Crossing thing was never what was intended in the general plan — ever." — Wheatley, per Building Salt Lake

Dec. 7, 2023 – May 2, 2024

The state says it will work

The Office of the Lieutenant Governor commissions LRB Public Finance Advisors to study whether the new city would be financially viable. LRB's study — the legal trigger required before the question can go to voters — concludes incorporation would "likely result in at least a five percent budget surplus."

Nov. 5, 2024

Voters approve incorporation

Ogden Valley City is approved by ballot. The incorporation was sponsored by six residents: Mark Ferrin (primary sponsor), Richard Webb, Brandi Hammond, Jeanie Wendell, Shanna Francis, and Nick Dahlkamp. Three of the six — Hammond, Wendell, and Francis — also sat on the committee that drew the city's boundary, which excludes both Powder Mountain and Snowbasin from the new city. Nordic Valley, whose land sits inside the boundary, is included.

Jan. 2, 2026

The city is born

Ogden Valley City officially incorporates. It has no in-house planning department and no in-house engineering department. It contracts with J-U-B Engineers for both — the same firm that also does infrastructure work directly for Powder Mountain, one of the two resorts the boundary excluded.

2026

The 2016 plan, reborn

Ogden Valley City's draft general plan — funded through a Wasatch Front Regional Council planning grant — turns out to be the 2016 Weber County plan, carried forward almost unchanged. Zions Bank Public Finance and Steve Waldrip are still on its credited acknowledgments page.

Nov. 20, 2024

The county doesn't wait

Fifteen days after voters approve incorporating a new city, Weber County creates the Nordic Village Public Infrastructure District on land that will sit inside that city's boundary — without waiting for the city government, which would eventually have jurisdiction over that land, to exist and have any say.

Nov. 2024 – Jan. 2026

Thirteen months with no one watching

Between the incorporation vote and the new city government being seated, Weber County approves the major financial commitments the city inherits — before the city exists to have any say in them.

  • Weber County approves the Nordic Village PID's Series 2025 bonds ($39.38M)
  • The county's Community Reinvestment Agency signs the interlocal agreement committing the Weber School District, Ogden Valley Fire District, and Weber Basin Water Conservancy District's future tax revenue growth for 15 years
2026, ongoing

The pattern continues under the new government

Cobabe Ranch — a new Lewis/Dunleavy development — launches inside Ogden Valley City limits. Two homeowners associations are formed in May 2026. No public disclosure of the project has come from Mayor Wampler.

July 14, 2026

A court voids the city's taxing authority

Third District Court rules against Ogden Valley City's taxing authority — because the city missed a January 1 incorporation-certificate deadline by one day. The city appeals to the Utah Supreme Court. FY27 runs on an interim, no-increase budget while the appeal is pending; even a Supreme Court win may arrive too late for the December 2026 property tax collection window.

Aug. 18, 2026

The surplus that wasn't

The Ogden Valley City Council votes 3-2 to conditionally adopt a budget with a 512.6% property tax increase, contingent on winning a truth-in-taxation appeal — a vote that took place while the city's own taxing authority was under the cloud of the July 14 ruling. The city's own financial advisors cite an overly optimistic post-COVID growth assumption baked into the original planning — the same kind of assumption LRB's feasibility study had relied on two years earlier.

  • For: Kay Hoogland, Don Hickman, Tia Shaw · Against: Peggy Dooling Baker, Chad Booth
  • The city's own Property Tax Impact Schedule shows reserves would differ by only about $14,000 whether the tax passes or not — and names a workable alternative (a transportation utility fee plus keeping the municipal energy tax) that would cover most of the gap without the increase
  • Council Member Kay Hoogland manages the city's legal-vendor relationship on the truth-in-taxation appeal, is its most vocal public advocate, and cast a yes vote on the tax resolution that litigation's outcome would fund — three roles with no rule requiring any separation between them

What the state promised vs. what happened

The feasibility study, Dec. 2023
+5% surplus

LRB Public Finance Advisors' legal finding, required under Utah Code §10-2a-205, that let the incorporation question reach the ballot.

The city's own numbers, 2026
$1.25M–2.2M shortfall

The gap the city's financial advisors say the 512.6% tax hike is meant to cover, roughly two years after incorporation.

The same names, more than once

THE OVERRIDE PATTERN

Gage Froerer

Weber County Commission Chair. Led the public defense of the Eden Crossing override in 2023. Separately documented with undisclosed real-estate interests connected to the Nordic Village development — the same PID whose land sits inside Ogden Valley City's boundary while its tax increment is diverted away from the city and other public taxing entities for fifteen years.

THE LAND ITSELF

John Lewis and Gage Froerer both had ownership ties to the land inside the PID

Before the four Nordic Valley parcels transferred into the PID, they were held by Nordic Valley Land Associates, LLC — managed in part by Froerer's own J&G Investment Partnership, LLC. The land transferred, at zero consideration, into Nordic Village Venture LLC — the entity now developing inside the PID, in which John Lewis holds a 5% ownership stake. Two people with documented financial ties to the land itself sat on, or influenced, the public bodies that approved turning it into a tax-diverting public infrastructure district.

THE BOUNDARY

Brandi Hammond, Shanna Francis & Jeanie Wendell

Three of the six named Ogden Valley City incorporation sponsors. All three were central drivers of incorporation and sat on the committee that drew the city's boundary — the same boundary that excluded Powder Mountain and Snowbasin from the new city's tax base. Hammond is now President of Powder Mountain. No public record supports any claim that the resorts asked to be excluded; the boundary was the committee's own decision.

FROM PLANNING COMMISSION TO STATE OFFICE

Steve Waldrip

Sat on the Weber County Planning Commission that adopted the 2016 general plan. He's now Gov. Cox's senior housing adviser — the same role in which he has publicly threatened to cut Wasatch County's funding over local opposition to state growth policy, and has pushed Public Infrastructure Districts statewide.

ONE FIRM, EVERY SEAT

J-U-B Engineers

Writes Ogden Valley City's general plan. Serves as the city's own Deputy City Engineer. Does infrastructure work directly for Powder Mountain, the resort the incorporation boundary left out. Full case file linked below.

THREE ROLES, NO SEPARATION

Kay Hoogland

Council member formally assigned to manage the city's legal-vendor relationship on the truth-in-taxation appeal. Also its most public and vocal advocate for pursuing the litigation. Also a yes vote on the tax resolution the litigation's outcome funds. Utah's conflict-of-interest law is keyed to financial interest, not this kind of overlap — nothing requires these three roles to be held by different people.

Who was against it

Incorporation passed 2,927 to 1,597 on Nov. 5, 2024. The organized opposition wasn't residents who preferred the status quo — it was the same developer network that had just won the Eden Crossing override, working to keep the valley under county control rather than a new, more locally accountable city.

THE OPPOSITION COMMITTEE

Keep Our Valley Free (KOVF)

A Political Issues Committee that ran a real campaign against incorporation — yard signs, printed materials, digital advertising, and a community donation.

  • Funded by: Utah Forward PAC ($2,427.20, Jan. 9, 2024); Wolf Creek Resort, a John Lewis-controlled entity ($5,912.70 total, two contributions); personal contributions from Laura Warburton and John Lewis
  • Managed by: Laura Warburton — the same person who managed the separate PAC that hired Zions Bank for the Nordic Village feasibility study, and who sat on the Planning Commission that approved Eden Crossing without disclosing her ties to its developer
  • Spent on: Wright Signs ($769.60), Bell Printing ($3,154.94), Taradel digital ads ($3,397.46), a feasibility-study-related payment ($4,218.75), and a $723.69 community donation
  • Records amended to delete filed transactions on Sept. 20, 2024 and Nov. 9, 2024
  • Dissolved Nov. 9, 2024 — four days after the vote it lost. Its filing: "We no longer wish to be recognized as an active Political Issues Committee... The balance of our account is zero."
THE MAYOR'S OWN FAMILY

Wampler Family WCRH, LLC

An active Utah LLC, formed 2018. Its registered agent is John Lewis, listed at his own Wolf Creek Resort address. Its Governing Person is Atlee Wampler — the father of Janet Wampler, Ogden Valley City's first mayor. The developer whose project became "the last straw" for incorporation, and whose network funded the opposition to it, is the registered agent for an LLC governed by the sitting mayor's own father.

The same developer network that benefited from the county's Eden Crossing override also funded the effort to keep the valley under that same county's control — a fuller picture of "who wanted what" than either side of the incorporation debate presented publicly at the time.
